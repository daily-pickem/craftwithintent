Whether you are an industry veteran leveling up, a white-collar builder navigating layoffs, or a recent CS graduate facing a tight market, the temptation with AI is to show volume: ten weekend landing pages that never met a real user.

Hiring bars have already adjusted to that noise. Anyone can ask a model for a dashboard. Very few candidates can walk a team through why they cut a feature, how they kept per-user costs near zero, and which real-world failure broke their first assumption.

## What stands up in a review

- **Clear problem scoping:** Showing what you explicitly refused to build in v1.
- **Testable architecture:** Isolating core logic with replay tests instead of relying on manual clicks.
- **Honest postmortems:** Documenting the ugly edge cases you caught in production and how you changed the harness so they never recur.

Those exact habits are what power both a sustainable indie product and a standout engineering or product interview.
